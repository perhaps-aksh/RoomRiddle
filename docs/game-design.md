# Game Design

## Experience
The TV is a cinematic stage viewed from the couch. Phones are personal investigation devices. They're more than a mirror of the TV:

> **TV:** "A pocket watch was discovered beneath seat 7."
>
> **Phone:** EVIDENCE · POCKET WATCH · Engraved: "E. Caldwell" · Time: 11:47 PM

## Session flow
**TV:** Home → Mystery Library → Create Room (QR + code) → Lobby → Intro → Investigation / Puzzles → Final Decision → Ending → Results

**Phone:** Scan QR → Join Room → Nickname/Avatar → Lobby → Investigate → Evidence → Solve → Submit Answers → Vote → Ending reveal

## Mystery structure (per mystery, pre-authored)
Metadata, characters, scenes, dialogue, clues and evidence, puzzles with accepted answers, progressive hints (3 levels), unlock conditions and dependencies, choices and voting, scoring, timers, endings, assets.

## Answer validation
Deterministic types: exact text, normalized text (case/whitespace/formatting), numeric, code, multiple choice, sequence, combination, selected evidence. No fuzzy or AI matching.

## First mystery: The Last Train (draft, full authoring in Phase 3)
A stormy night. A train approaches midnight. The conductor has vanished, the emergency brake is locked, and something happened at **11:47 PM**.

- Planned: 4 puzzles, several clues, 3 progressive hints per puzzle, a final team vote, 3 endings.
- Draft puzzle ideas: *The Passenger's Last Note* (symbols from a luggage tag), *The Broken Timetable* (station sequence), *The Conductor's Cipher* (decoded phrase), then a code puzzle and the final decision.
- Draft endings: **The Rescue**, **The Disappearance**, **The Midnight Secret** (the timer runs out or the system isn't restored).
- **Open question:** the Phase 3 flow lists four puzzles *followed by* a separate final decision. The earlier draft counted the decision as puzzle 4. Settle this before authoring.
